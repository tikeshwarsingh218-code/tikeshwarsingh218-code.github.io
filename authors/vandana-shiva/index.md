# Vandana Shiva

Vandana Shiva is an Indian environmental thinker, writer, activist, and scholar whose work focuses on ecology, agriculture, biodiversity, food systems, intellectual property, and the relationship between environmental change and social justice.

She was trained as a physicist and completed a PhD in the philosophy of quantum theory at the University of Western Ontario. She later moved into interdisciplinary research on science, technology, environmental policy, and agriculture.

In 1982, Shiva founded the Research Foundation for Science, Technology and Ecology. She later founded Navdanya, a movement focused on biodiversity conservation, seed sovereignty, and the rights of small farmers. Her work has also addressed the effects of agricultural technologies, patents, and industrial farming on farmers and local communities. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Shiva is the author of numerous books, including *Staying Alive*, *Monocultures of the Mind*, *Soil Not Oil*, *Who Really Feeds the World?*, and *The Violence of the Green Revolution*. Her writing combines environmental analysis with arguments about agriculture, political economy, technology, and social change. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

*The Violence of the Green Revolution: Third World Agriculture, Ecology and Politics*, first published in 1991, examines the effects of Green Revolution agriculture in India. The book discusses monoculture, genetic diversity, chemical fertilizers, irrigation, soil fertility, and the social and political consequences of agricultural transformation, particularly in Punjab. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Shiva has received numerous international awards for her work on environmental and social issues, including the Right Livelihood Award in 1993. She continues to work through Navdanya on biodiversity, seed conservation, ecological agriculture, and food sovereignty. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
