# Jean Drèze

Jean Drèze is a development economist whose research and writing focus on economic and social development, particularly in India. He has taught at the London School of Economics and the Delhi School of Economics and has been a Visiting Professor at Ranchi University.

His work has examined a wide range of issues including poverty, hunger, nutrition, education, health care, employment, social security, and public policy. He has combined economic analysis with field research and close attention to the experiences of people affected by public programmes and social policies.

Drèze has been closely associated with research and public discussion on India's social policy. His work has contributed to debates around the right to food, employment guarantees, school meals, the public distribution system, health care, and elementary education.

He is the co-author, with Amartya Sen, of *Hunger and Public Action* and *An Uncertain Glory: India and Its Contradictions*. He is also the author of *Sense and Solidarity: Jholawala Economics for Everyone*, which brings together essays on social development and public policy in India. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/35106))

*Sense and Solidarity* discusses issues including poverty, school meals, health care, child development, employment guarantees, food security, the public distribution system, corporate power, and social policy. The book also argues for an approach to development economics in which research, practical experience, and democratic action are closely connected. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/35106/chapter/299184200))

Drèze's work has therefore combined economic research with field experience and engagement with questions of social policy, public institutions, and the conditions of everyday life.
