# Arundhati Roy

Arundhati Roy is an Indian writer whose work spans fiction, essays, political writing, and memoir. Her first novel, *The God of Small Things*, was published in 1997 and won the Booker Prize that year. Her second novel, *The Ministry of Utmost Happiness*, was published in 2017 and was longlisted for the Booker Prize. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Roy has also written extensively on politics, society, inequality, democracy, conflict, and contemporary India. Her nonfiction includes *The End of Imagination*, *The Algebra of Infinite Justice*, *Field Notes on Democracy*, *Broken Republic*, *Capitalism: A Ghost Story*, *The Doctor and the Saint*, *My Seditious Heart*, and *Azadi*. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Her writing often combines literary essays with political and social commentary. Her nonfiction has addressed questions of power, state institutions, inequality, violence, democracy, and the effects of political and economic change.

In 2023, Roy received the European Essay Prize for lifetime achievement, and in 2024 she received the PEN Pinter Prize. She lives in Delhi. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Her memoir *Mother Mary Comes to Me*, published in 2025, explores her relationship with her mother, Mary Roy, and traces how her childhood, family life, and experiences shaped her development as a writer. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
