# James C. Scott

James C. Scott (1936–2024) was an American political scientist and anthropologist whose work focused on political economy, agrarian societies, peasant politics, state power, resistance, and Southeast Asia. He was a professor at Yale University, where he held appointments in political science, anthropology, and environmental studies. He also founded and directed Yale's Program in Agrarian Studies. ([yale.edu](https://politicalscience.yale.edu/people/james-scott))

Scott's research was strongly grounded in fieldwork and historical analysis. His work examined how ordinary people respond to systems of political and economic power, including the ways in which peasants and other subordinate groups resist, negotiate with, or adapt to state authority and social hierarchies.

His major books include *The Moral Economy of the Peasant*, *Weapons of the Weak*, *Domination and the Arts of Resistance*, *Seeing Like a State*, *The Art of Not Being Governed*, and *Against the Grain*. His research crossed the boundaries of political science, anthropology, history, geography, and development studies. ([yale.edu](https://politicalscience.yale.edu/people/james-scott))

*Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to Improve the Human Condition Have Failed*, first published in 1998, examined why large-scale state planning can fail when governments simplify complex societies and environments in order to make them easier to administer. The book emphasizes the importance of local, practical knowledge that may be lost when centralized schemes impose standardized systems. ([Yale University Press](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300078152/seeing-like-a-state/))

Scott's later work extended these interests to questions of state formation, agriculture, political autonomy, and societies that remained relatively outside the control of centralized states. His scholarship had a wide influence across the social sciences, particularly in studies of development, governance, agrarian change, and political resistance.
