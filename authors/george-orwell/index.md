# George Orwell

George Orwell was the pen name of Eric Arthur Blair (1903–1950), an English novelist, essayist, journalist, and critic. He was born in Motihari, India, and spent much of his early life in England.

Before becoming a full-time writer, Orwell served as a police officer in Burma. His experiences there contributed to his opposition to imperialism and later informed some of his writing about colonialism and power.

Orwell wrote extensively about poverty, war, political power, social inequality, language, and the relationship between individuals and institutions. His nonfiction includes *Down and Out in Paris and London*, *The Road to Wigan Pier*, and *Homage to Catalonia*.

He is best known for *Animal Farm* (1945) and *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (1949). *Animal Farm* is a political satire about a revolution that develops into a new system of domination, while *Nineteen Eighty-Four* examines surveillance, political control, propaganda, and the manipulation of language. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Orwell's writing combined political and social commentary with a strong emphasis on clear and direct language. His essays and novels have continued to influence discussions of political power, freedom, propaganda, and the use of language in public life.
