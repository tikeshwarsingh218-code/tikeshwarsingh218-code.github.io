# Milton Friedman

Milton Friedman (1912–2006) was an American economist and one of the leading figures in twentieth-century monetary economics. He taught at the University of Chicago from 1946 to 1976 and later served as a senior research fellow at the Hoover Institution. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Friedman received the 1976 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his contributions to consumption analysis, monetary history and theory, and his analysis of stabilization policy. His work made important contributions to monetary economics, including the study of the relationship between money supply, economic activity, and inflation. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

He became closely associated with the Chicago School of economics and was an influential advocate of monetary explanations of inflation and economic fluctuations. His research also covered consumption, business cycles, monetary policy, and international economics. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Friedman also wrote extensively for a wider audience about economic policy and individual freedom. His books include *Capitalism and Freedom*, written with Rose D. Friedman and first published in 1962, and *Free to Choose*, also written with Rose Friedman. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

*Capitalism and Freedom* discusses the relationship between economic and political freedom and covers subjects including monetary policy, fiscal policy, international trade, education, inequality, and poverty. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
